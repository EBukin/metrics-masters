Appendix F — Fitting spatial panels

Maximum likelihood, generalised moments, fixed and random effects

Code: fixed effects, random effects and serial correlation too. Related notes: spatial model choice and spatial weights.

F.1 Why least squares is not enough

A spatial error model could be fitted by least squares: the slopes would be consistent, only their standard errors wrong. A spatial lag model could not, since \(Wy\) is endogenous. Both need an estimator that knows about W, and two families supply it. Maximum likelihood writes down the joint normal density of all NT observations, with the determinant that the spatial transformation adds, and maximises it. Generalised moments uses the fact that the innovations are uncorrelated with their own spatial lags, solves a small set of moment equations for λ, and finishes with feasible GLS. The first is efficient under normality and inconsistent under heteroskedasticity of unknown form (Lin and Lee 2010; Pesaran 2015, 807); the second needs neither normality nor a determinant, and gives no standard error for the spatial parameter.

F.2 1. The likelihood and its Jacobian

For the pooled spatial error model, with \(B = I_N - \lambda W\) and \(u = y - X\beta\) (Anselin 1988, ch. 6; Croissant and Millo 2019, sec. 10.2.3):

\[ \ln L = -\frac{NT}{2}\ln(2\pi\sigma^2) + T\ln|B| - \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\, u'(I_T \otimes B'B)\,u . \]

The term \(T \ln|B| = T \sum_i \ln(1 - \lambda\omega_i)\), with ω the eigenvalues of W, is the Jacobian of the transformation from the innovations to the observed errors; without it the likelihood would push λ to the edge of its range. For the spatial lag model the transformation is on the outcome, \(A y = X\beta + u\) with \(A = I_N - \rho W\), and the Jacobian is \(T\ln|A|\). spml computes the eigenvalues once and the sum at every step of nlminb; for N in the thousands it switches to sparse or approximate determinants (Millo and Piras 2012, sec. 5).

F.3 2. Fixed effects: transformation or correction

Within estimation demeans every variable by unit. For a spatial model the demeaning has a side effect: the T demeaned errors of a unit are no longer independent, so the likelihood above is misspecified on the demeaned data (Croissant and Millo 2019, sec. 10.3.1). Lee and Yu (2010) give two ways out. The first replaces demeaning with an orthonormal transformation, a matrix that removes the unit mean and leaves T − 1 uncorrelated errors behind; the estimator is then consistent and asymptotically normal when either N or T grows (Pesaran 2015, 802–3). The second keeps demeaning and corrects the error variance after the fact (Lee and Yu 2010, sec. 3.2). splm takes the second route: spml(model = "within") is the pooled fit on demeaned data with the variance corrected, and the “either N or T” property of the first route does not transfer to it. Millo and Piras (2012, sec. 8.2) check by simulation that the practical difference is small.

F.4 3. Random effects: two covariances, twelve error structures

With a random unit effect the error is \(u_{it} = \mu_i + v_{it}\), and the question is where W enters. In Anselin’s specification only the period shock is spatial, \(v_{\cdot t} = \lambda W v_{\cdot t} + \varepsilon_{\cdot t}\), and with \(\phi = \sigma^2_\mu/\sigma^2_\varepsilon\) (Baltagi 2021, eq. 13.10; Croissant and Millo 2019, sec. 10.3.2.1)

\[ \Omega = \sigma^2_\varepsilon \big[\phi\,(J_T \otimes I_N) + I_T \otimes (B'B)^{-1}\big]. \]

In the specification of Kapoor et al. (2007) the unit effect passes through W as well, so the whole composite error is spatially filtered (Croissant and Millo 2019, sec. 10.3.2.2):

\[ \Omega = \sigma^2_\varepsilon\, (\phi J_T + I_T) \otimes (B'B)^{-1}. \]

spml(model = "random") calls these spatial.error = "b" and "kkp". Both are cases of the error menu of spreml, which composes three letters: sem for a spatial error, sr for a serially correlated remainder, \(\varepsilon_{it} = \psi\varepsilon_{i,t-1} + \eta_{it}\), and re for the random effect. "semsrre" holds all three (Millo 2014; Baltagi et al. 2007); "semsr" drops the effect; "sem2re" is the KKP form; "ols" is a plain regression, which with lag = TRUE is the pure spatial lag of the map section. The likelihood “is not well behaved”: nlminb from zero starting values is the default, initval = "estimate" starts from the restricted fits, and a singular Hessian means a parameter is not there to estimate, as on a panel from which the unit means have already been partialled out.

F.5 4. Generalised moments

Kelejian and Prucha (1999) estimate λ from three moment conditions on the innovations \(\varepsilon = B u\): their mean square equals \(\sigma^2\), the mean square of \(W\varepsilon\) equals \(\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(W'W)/N\), and \(\varepsilon\) and \(W\varepsilon\) are uncorrelated. Replace u by consistent residuals, solve the three equations in λ and \(\sigma^2\), and run GLS with the implied covariance. Kapoor et al. (2007) extend the conditions to six for the random-effects panel (Baltagi 2021, eq. 13.13), which can be used three ways: the first three only (moments = "initial", the default and the least efficient), all six weighted by their covariance under normality ("fullweights"), or all six with a simplified weight ("weights"). For fixed effects, Mutl and Pfaffermayr (2011) replace the OLS residuals by within two-stage least squares residuals and use the first three conditions. spgm wraps all of it, with method choosing among the within, between, GLS and error-component 2SLS estimators of Baltagi (2021, 401–4) when a lag is present. Two costs: no standard error for λ, so no significance test on it, and with a single regressor the summary() method of splm 1.6-5 fails, so the fit is read from coef() and $rho.

F.6 5. The tests that need only the restricted model

Three families of Lagrange multiplier test fit from the OLS residuals, so that the expensive model is estimated only once a test has asked for it.

Lag against error. The four statistics of Anselin et al. (1996), in the choice note.

Random effect and spatial error. Baltagi et al. (2003) derive the joint test of \(\sigma^2_\mu = 0\) and λ = 0 (LMH), the marginal tests of each (LM1, LM2), and the conditional tests of each allowing the other (CLMmu, CLMlambda); the conditional ones are the ones to read, since the joint test rejects for either reason (Croissant and Millo 2019, sec. 10.3.4.1).

Random effect, spatial error and serial correlation. Baltagi et al. (2007) add ψ as a third symptom, with a joint test J and the three conditional tests C.1 (λ given the other two), C.2 (ψ given the other two) and C.3 (\(\sigma^2_\mu\) given the other two). Each conditional test fits the model with the other two effects and tests the third from its score, which is why C.2 on rice takes a few seconds. Testing for any one of the three while ignoring the other two “leads to misleading results”; the full semsrre fit then puts magnitudes beside the tests.

Fixed against random. The spatial Hausman test of Mutl and Pfaffermayr (2011) compares the random-effects GLS slopes with the within slopes,

\[ H = (\hat\beta_{RE} - \hat\beta_{FE})'\, [\widehat V_{FE} - \widehat V_{RE}]^{-1}\, (\hat\beta_{RE} - \hat\beta_{FE}) \sim \chi^2_k , \]

(Millo and Piras 2012, eq. 32). sphtest fits both from a formula, by ML with errors = "BSK" or "KKP", or by GM with no error structure to choose; or takes two fitted objects. The statistic is conditional on the spatial model passed as spatial.model: on the rice panel it rejects random effects under the error model, χ² = 31, and does not under the lag model, χ² = 0.6. The verdict that counts is the one under the model the choice note selected.

F.7 6. Reading the output: the naming trap

Four conventions meet in this chapter, and two are each other’s reverse:

Where Spatial lag Spatial error
Pesaran (2015, eqs. 30.1, 30.4), and this book ρ λ
spml(model = "within"): coef() and the printed table lambda rho
spml(model = "random") and spreml lambda in $arcoef rho in $errcomp
Millo and Piras (2012), printed output rho lambda

Read a parameter by what it does. A within SEM fit has one spatial number in coef(), called rho, and it is the error parameter; a within SAR fit has one, called lambda, and it is the lag. Residuals of either come back stacked by period, then unit, and splm_residuals() puts them in panel order before any test touches them.

F.8 If / then

If Then
Errors may be non-normal or heteroskedastic GM; report that λ has no standard error
Normality is plausible and N is moderate ML; the Wald z on λ or ρ is then available
Fixed effects with a spatial lag spml(model = "within", lag = TRUE); the within correction, not the orthonormal transformation
The unit effect may itself be spatial Compare "b" with "kkp"; with a small φ it does not matter
The random-effects fit stops with a singular Hessian A parameter is absent; on partialled data, the unit effect is already gone
The error may carry three things bsjktest C.1 to C.3, then spreml(errors = "semsrre")
A printed rho and lambda Check which model it is before reading either

F.9 Open

  • Heteroskedasticity-robust panel GM (Lin and Lee 2010; Kelejian and Prucha 2010) is not implemented for panels; sphet (Piras 2010) is cross-sectional only.
  • The fixed-effects LM and LR tests of Debarsy and Ertur (2010) are not implemented.
  • Dynamic spatial panels have no estimator in R; the limits section states what that costs.
  • The within log-likelihoods that spml returns for lag and error fits are not on one scale, so a likelihood-ratio test between a within SAR and a within SEM cannot be read off them; run the comparison with random effects, as the random-effects section does.
