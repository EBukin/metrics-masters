Appendix D — Testing for spatial dependence

Ordered alternatives, local CD, and the randomised-W test

Code: does it follow the map and the residual checks in fixed effects. Related notes: cross-sectional dependence and spatial weights.

D.1 Two families of test

The tests for cross-sectional dependence split by whether the units are ordered (Pesaran 2015, 784). The CD test, the LM tests and the exponent α treat the units as an unordered set: they ask whether the units move together, and how pervasively, without a notion of who is near whom. On a map they detect whether anything exists, never whether it has spatial structure.

That is not a small gap. The implicit null of the CD test is weak dependence, α below 1/4 when N and T grow together (Pesaran 2015, 786), and spatial processes generate weak dependence (Pesaran 2015, sec. 30.3.3). From the other side, Pesaran (2015, 839) find the CD test to be the panel unit-root diagnostic most robust to spatial alternatives, which is to say it does not fire on them. A spatial panel can pass the CD test and still be spatially dependent in every residual. The ordered family exists for that case.

D.2 1. The ordered statistics

Put the map in. Pesaran (2015, eqs. 30.40–30.41) give two forms. The first is Moran’s I on the panel,

\[ CD_{\text{Moran}} = \frac{\sum_t \sum_{i \ne j} w_{ij}\, \hat u_{it}\hat u_{jt}} {\sqrt{\,\cdot\,}}, \]

the covariance of each residual with its neighbours’ average, scaled to a standard normal (Kelejian and Prucha 1998). For large N it is equivalent to the Lagrange multiplier test of Burridge (1980), which is the lme statistic of the lag-or-error section. The second is the local CD statistic: the CD average restricted to pairs that W links, with the weights kept,

\[ CD_{P,\text{local}} = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\sum_{i<j} w_{ij}}}\ \sum_{i<j} w_{ij}\,\hat\rho_{ij}. \]

What plm implements as pcdtest(w = ) is the binary version: w is passed through as.logical(), so every non-zero cell becomes 1 and the statistic averages the correlations of the linked pairs with equal weight (Croissant and Millo 2019, sec. 10.1.2.1). It tests the neighbourhood structure. The weighted form of eq. 30.41 and \(CD_{\text{Moran}}\) are not implemented in this book’s tools.

D.3 2. Why local CD over-reads a factor

Local CD inherits the CD test’s virtue, exact centring for any T, and adds one of its own: it has power on cross-sectionally demeaned data, where global CD is dead by construction (Croissant and Millo 2019, sec. 10.1.2.2). It also has a specific blind spot. If a factor moves every unit, every pair of units is correlated, neighbours included. Local CD then averages “a subset of spatially related pairs from a population of correlated ones”, and rejects, favouring a spatial reading of what is not spatial (Croissant and Millo 2019, sec. 10.1.2.2). On the raw house-price index it gives z = 37, with the national force still in the data; on the CCEMG residuals it gives z = 28, with the force out. Only the second number is evidence about geography, and nothing in the first number says so.

D.4 3. The randomised-W test

Millo (2017) turns the blind spot into a test. Under the null that the map does not matter, the units are exchangeable: hand each state a random set of “neighbours” by permuting the order of the units, and the local statistic should look the same. Compute it under B shuffled maps, \(\tau_1, \dots, \tau_B\), and compare with the value under the true map, τ. The one-sided pseudo-p-value, for the usual alternative of positive spatial dependence, is (Croissant and Millo 2019, eq. 10.1)

\[ p^{+} = \frac{1 + \sum_{h=1}^{B} \mathbf{1}[\tau_h \ge \tau]}{B + 1}; \]

the two-sided version takes absolute values of cross-sectionally demeaned data, and the asymmetric version counts each tail separately (Croissant and Millo 2019, eqs. 10.2–10.3). rwtest defaults to alternative = "twosided" and to test = "rho", the average correlation of the linked pairs; "cd" and "sclm" use the local CD and scaled-LM statistics instead.

The test is robust to what local CD is not. A factor moves the whole distribution of shuffled statistics, true map included, so it cancels in the comparison; serial correlation does the same. That is why Croissant and Millo (2019, sec. 10.1.2.2) run it on the MG residuals, with the factor still in them, and get the same p = 0.002 as on the raw index. It is the test that displaces local CD as the primary tool, and local CD becomes the statistic that the shuffle is applied to.

Its limits are the ones its construction implies. With B = 999 the smallest p-value is 0.001 and the pile has 999 points; with the default B = 99 it is coarse. It reads W through as.logical() like pcdtest, so it never tests a weighting scheme. And it tests one order of neighbourhood at a time; order = 2 shuffles the second-order map.

D.5 4. Before and after the fit

The same two tests serve twice (Croissant and Millo 2019, sec. 10.1.2). On the raw data, or on residuals from which a factor has been removed, they say whether a spatial model is called for. On the residuals of the spatial model, they say whether it did its job. The second use has a trap. A spatial error model’s residuals \(u = y - X\hat\beta\) are spatially correlated by construction: the model says \(u = (I - \lambda W)^{-1}\varepsilon\), so a residual test on \(u\) rejects whenever the model is right. What the model claims to be free of spatial correlation is \(\varepsilon = (I - \hat\lambda W)u\), period by period, and that is what the check must test. On the transformed house-price panel the local CD of \(u\) is 32 and that of \(\varepsilon\) is 0.5; on rice, 47 and −0.5. A spatial lag model’s residuals \(y - \hat\rho Wy - X\hat\beta\) are already the innovations, and need no filter.

D.6 If / then

If Then
CD does not reject and the units sit on a map Nothing is settled; run local CD and the shuffle (Pesaran 2015, 839)
Local CD rejects on raw data with α near 1 A factor is in the way; defactor first, or read the shuffle, never local CD alone
The shuffle rejects on MG residuals The dependence follows the map, factor or no factor
The shuffle does not reject Space adds nothing; the dependence is a factor, or noise; robust standard errors
Checking a spatial error fit Test the filtered innovations \((I - \hat\lambda W)u\), not \(u\)
Checking a spatial lag fit Test the residuals as they are
Negative spatial dependence is plausible alternative = "symmetric" or the asymmetric form; the one-sided test can miss it
W carries distances Both tests drop them; report that only the structure was tested

D.7 Open

  • The weighted local CD and \(CD_{\text{Moran}}\) of Pesaran (2015, eqs. 30.40–30.41) are not implemented; a hand-coded version would have no target beyond agreement with the binary one.
  • Robinson’s quadratic forms (Pesaran 2015, 815) are not implemented.
  • Higher orders. The shuffle at order = 2 tests the neighbours-of-neighbours map, which is the natural follow-up when the first-order test rejects and the question is how far the dependence reaches. No published number exists for it on either panel.
