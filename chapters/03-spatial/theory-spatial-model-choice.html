Appendix E — Spatial model choice

SAR, SEM, SARAR and Durbin: what each claims, and how to choose

Code: lag or error and fixed effects. Related notes: spatial weights, fitting spatial panels and direct and indirect effects.

E.1 Two claims about the world

A spatial lag says that a neighbour’s outcome moves yours: a tax rate set with an eye on the next state’s, a disease that spreads, a price that buyers compare across a border. A spatial error says that only the shocks are shared: the weather that hits a whole valley, a measurement error that is local, a variable the model left out that happens to cluster on the map. The first is a mechanism; the second is a nuisance that has to be allowed for, or, as Holly et al. (2010) read it for house prices, the diffusion of a shock that is itself worth measuring (Croissant and Millo 2019, sec. 10.2.2). They are not two ways to fit one curve. They are different statements about what would happen if one unit’s outcome were changed, and the effects note spells out the difference.

E.2 1. The specifications

Stack the N units of period t (Pesaran 2015, eqs. 30.1–30.9; Baltagi 2021, 401–6):

Name Equation Claim
SAR, spatial lag \(y_t = \rho W y_t + X_t\beta + u_t\) outcomes move outcomes
SEM, spatial error \(y_t = X_t\beta + u_t,\ u_t = \lambda W u_t + \varepsilon_t\) shocks are shared
SARAR both, with \(W_1\) and \(W_2\) possibly different both
SDM, Durbin \(y_t = \rho W y_t + X_t\beta + W X_t\theta + u_t\) neighbours’ regressors matter too

The reduced forms say what each does to an estimate. Under SAR, \(y_t = (I - \rho W)^{-1}(X_t\beta + u_t)\): \(Wy_t\) contains \(u_t\) and is endogenous, so least squares on it, pooled or within, is inconsistent (Pesaran 2015, 800; Baltagi 2021, 401). Under SEM, \(u_t = (I - \lambda W)^{-1}\varepsilon_t\): the regressors stay exogenous, and least squares is consistent and merely inefficient, with standard errors that are wrong. Pesaran (2015, 801) put the consequence in one line: under spatial errors, fixed and random effects are \(\sqrt{NT}\)-consistent, and spatial modelling buys efficiency, not consistency.

The Durbin term \(WX_t\) costs nothing in estimation: \(WX_t\) is as exogenous as \(X_t\), and splm::slag() adds it to any formula. What it changes is the reading of the coefficients, since the effect of \(x_k\) on unit i now runs through \(\beta_k\) and \(\theta_k\) together (Baltagi 2021, 406; LeSage and Pace 2009, sec. 2.7).

E.3 2. The Lagrange multiplier battery

The tests of Anselin et al. (1996) need only the non-spatial fit. With residuals e, the error test is

\[ LM_\lambda = \frac{(e'We/\hat\sigma^2)^2}{T_W}, \qquad T_W = \operatorname{tr}(W'W + W^2), \]

and the lag test replaces \(e'We\) with \(e'Wy\) and \(T_W\) with a term that also involves the fitted values. Each is \(\chi^2_1\) under its null, and each rejects under the other’s alternative, because an omitted lag leaves spatially correlated residuals and an omitted error makes \(Wy\) look informative. The robust versions subtract from each statistic the part the other effect would contribute (Anselin et al. 1996, eqs. 10, 13):

\[ RLM_\lambda = \frac{\big(e'We/\hat\sigma^2 - T_W\, \tilde J^{-1}\, e'Wy/\hat\sigma^2\big)^2} {T_W\,(1 - T_W \tilde J^{-1})}, \]

with \(\tilde J\) the information term of the lag test. The adjustment is exact for a local departure, a small value of the other parameter, and “can behave suboptimally” when both effects are large (Croissant and Millo 2019, sec. 10.3.4.2). The reading rule that follows is in two parts. When a plain test rejects and its robust version does not, the signal was the other effect’s shadow, which is the rice case: lml = 39, rlml = 0.17. When both robust tests reject strongly, the tests have left their design range, which is the house-price case: rlml = 32, rlme = 28. The answer then comes from the fits.

slmtest implements the four for a pooled model, which is what Anselin et al. (1996) derived. With unit effects, pass model = "within" or demean the variables with plm::Within() in the formula; the lag-or-error section does both.

E.4 3. The encompassing model as arbiter

When the robust tests do not settle it, fit SARAR and read it two ways (Croissant and Millo 2019, sec. 10.3.4.2). A Wald z on each spatial parameter needs no robustification, since the other effect is in the model. A likelihood ratio between SARAR and the nested SAR or SEM, \(2[\ln L_{\text{SARAR}} - \ln L_{\text{SEM}}] \sim \chi^2_1\), asks whether the lag adds anything given the error, and the reverse. On rice with random effects the lag given the error has p = 0.91: the error is the whole story.

The arbiter has one weakness. With the same W in both places, a SARAR model is close to unidentified: a positive error and a negative lag can reproduce the covariance of a plain SEM, and the two parameters trade off against each other with large standard errors (Elhorst 2014, ch. 2; LeSage and Pace 2009, ch. 2). The transformed house-price panel shows it, with a lag of −0.52 and an error of 0.87 where the separate fits gave 0.65 and 0.69. In that case the encompassing model reports two numbers that mean less than either separate one, and the honest write-up gives SEM and SAR side by side with their impacts.

E.5 4. The unit effect’s own spatial structure

With random effects there is a further choice (Baltagi 2021, 393–95; Pesaran 2015, 803). In Anselin’s specification the unit effect is plain and only the period shock passes through \((I - \lambda W)^{-1}\); the spillovers are time-varying. In the specification of Kapoor et al. (2007) the unit effect passes through it too, so a good farmer raises the neighbours’ output in every season; the spillovers are time-invariant as well. Baltagi et al. (2013) nest both in a model with three matrices. The two differ only through the variance ratio of the unit effect; when it is small, as on rice, the fits are the same to the second decimal, and the random-effects section shows it. Whether the unit effect may be random at all is the spatial Hausman test’s question, and its answer depends on the spatial model it is asked under: on rice it rejects under the error model and not under the lag model.

E.6 If / then

If Then
The question is a mechanism, outcomes moving outcomes SAR or SDM, and report impacts, not β
The question is a slope, with shocks that cluster SEM; β is the effect, and the fit buys precision
Plain lag test rejects, robust lag test does not The lag was the error’s shadow; SEM
Both robust tests reject strongly Fit SEM, SAR and SARAR; if SARAR’s parameters flip sign, report the two separate fits
Neighbours’ regressors plausibly matter Add \(WX\) with splm::slag(); the effects change, the estimation does not
Unit effects are random and large Anselin against KKP matters; with a small variance ratio it does not
A shock should stop at the first neighbour The error form needed is not implemented; state it

E.7 Open

  • SMA and SEC errors (Pesaran 2015, eqs. 30.5–30.6) are not in splm, which fits the autoregressive error only.
  • The fixed-effects LM and LR tests of Debarsy and Ertur (2010), joint, marginal and conditional, are not implemented; their LR structure can be rebuilt from the log-likelihoods of nested spml fits, but the within log-likelihoods of splm are not on one scale across lag and error fits, so the comparison has to run with random effects.
  • A test for the Durbin term beyond the Wald z on \(\theta\) is not covered here.
